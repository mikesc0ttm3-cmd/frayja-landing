Frayja: Privacy-First Messaging & File Transfer for the Post-Cloud Era

A sovereign, privacy-first platform for users who demand control and security.

Vision & Purpose

  • Sovereign, privacy-first platform for messaging and file transfer
  • Leverages PKI and IPFS
  • No phone numbers, no cloud sync, no metadata exposure
  • Desktop-only experience for maximum user control
  • Mythic branding & intuitive UX for accessible advanced security
  • Currently raising €2 million in pre-seed funding
  • Monetization via freemium and enterprise offerings

Executive Q&A Highlights

  • Why Now? The shift to a post-cloud era makes privacy-first solutions essential. Frayja’s decentralized approach meets rising demand for sovereignty and security.
  • Usability for All: Abstracts technical complexity—secure messaging and file transfer with simple, intuitive controls. Key generation and file signing are seamless and automatic.
  • Desktop-Only Strategy: Avoids OS-level surveillance and app store constraints. Mobile clients may come later, but only if privacy can be guaranteed.
  • Go-to-Market: Mythic branding, community-first engagement, and partnerships build momentum through clarity and storytelling.
  • Team: Founder-led, with expertise in privacy architecture and branding. Plans to onboard a technical cofounder or studio partner with PKI, IPFS, and UX experience.
  • Business Model: Freemium for personal use, Pro for encrypted team collaboration, and Enterprise licensing for sovereign infrastructure. Exploring tokenless staking and white-label licensing for privacy-first institutions.

Payment System

  • Polygon (MATIC): Fast, low-fee payments for subscriptions and upgrades, enabling global access without banking barriers.
  • Rune Tokens: Community staking for premium features, governance, and early access—no KYC or centralized account management.
  • Frayja’s payment system is as privacy-preserving as its core architecture.

Security Trends & Real-World Risks

  • Recent Messaging App Hacks: High-profile breaches in 2025 (WhatsApp, Signal, Apple Messages, Android apps) highlight the vulnerabilities of centralized platforms and the need for sovereign, privacy-first solutions.
  • Email Risks: Email remains insecure, with major breaches and credential leaks exposing sensitive data. Organizations often fail to implement proper encryption and access controls.
